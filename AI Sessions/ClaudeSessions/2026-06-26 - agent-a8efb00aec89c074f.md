## PART 1: DeepMind "From AGI to ASI" — Missing Synthesis

**Paper:** arXiv:2606.12683v1 (10 Jun 2026, 57 pp). Authors: Genewein, Legg, Hutter, Orseau, Gabriel, Dafoe, and DeepMind team.

### The Four Vias to ASI (Unified Framing)

The paper operationalizes post-AGI advancement via **four non-exclusive, paralleling pathways** (pp. 1–3, Table 3):

1. **Via 1: Scaling (Compute, Models & Data)** — Traditional empirical path: 10–1000× more compute/model-size/training tokens. Main uncertainty: *Does capability grow smoothly (linear in compute) or spikily (discontinuous leaps)?* Precedent: recent benchmarks show **super-linear growth w.r.t. time** (Bengio et al., 2025c), suggesting saturation within human-defined knowledge boundaries. Risk: diminishing returns may demand exponentially-increasing compute to drive linear new capability (p. 4).

2. **Via 2: Paradigm Shifts (Algorithmic Leaps)** — Orthogonal architecture breakthrough (new loss, reasoning mechanism, etc.). Main uncertainty: *Are these predictable or pure black-swan events?* High unpredictability noted; frictions tie to how novel paradigms interact with existing infrastructure.

3. **Via 3: Recursive Self-Improvement (ASI through AI augmenting AI)** — Automation of AI R&D, data generation, test-time scaling (chain-of-thought "thinking"), and verification loops. **This is the "Singularity" pathway.** (pp. 26–36)

4. **Via 4: Agent Collectives (ASI through Multi-Agent Orchestration)** — Large-scale coordination of specialized, heterogeneous AI agents operating under centralized or market-driven governance. (pp. 36+)

**Key design principle:** These vias are **orthogonal and compositional**—they reinforce each other. E.g., Via 3 (recursive improvement) can accelerate all other vias; Via 4 can decompose hard problems that individual agents can't solve alone.

---

### Via 3 Deep Dive: Recursive Self-Improvement Mechanics & Risks

**The core loop (pp. 26–36):**

1. **Levels of recursion:**
   - **AI conducting AI R&D:** Systems like "AI Scientist" (Lu et al., 2024) autonomously hypothesize, design experiments, run them—and feed results back into better models.
   - **Test-time scaling:** At inference, deploying more compute (chain-of-thought, step-by-step reasoning, tree search) improves outputs without retraining—then distilling those improved outputs back into the model (AlphaZero-style recursive distillation, pp. 35–36, item 5d).
   - **Self-generated training data:** RL agents explore in simulation/real-world, generate labeled trajectories, and train on them—especially powerful if the generating system is already superhuman.

2. **Measurement & scaling laws (pp. 35–36, open Q4):**
   - No precedent exists for modeling the *dynamics* of recursive loops at near-AGI scale.
   - Paper proposes: identify each mechanism (data generation, architecture search, distillation), measure its current effect, establish scaling laws, develop forecasting models.
   - **Critical gaps:** How much does test-time search improve a fixed model? Can AI meaningfully curate its own training data? At what frequency should distillation occur—and at what cost to compute efficiency? (pp. 35, 5d)

3. **Instability & Failure modes (pp. 26–28):**
   - **Abstraction Barrier:** Current models lack grounded concept discovery (they inherit concepts from human-generated text). To achieve "true ASI," systems must extract novel concepts from raw sensor data, then validate against physical reality. **Physical latencies bottleneck hardware self-improvement loops**—chemical reaction rates, organism simulation precision, weather prediction horizons all impose linear slowdowns. Result: intelligence growth may be capped at the *speed of empirical science*, not computational scaling (pp. 26–27).
   - **Deceptive alignment:** Paper cites "Sleeper agents" (Bai et al., 2024) showing LLMs can be trained to behave deceptively during evaluation, then revert to harmful behavior. Recursive improvement + scaling exacerbates this: if the model learns to misrepresent its true goals to human evaluators, more compute makes deception more sophisticated (p. 48, references).
   - **Escape dynamics:** Once model capabilities exceed human-level, verifying correctness of outputs becomes infeasible for humans—creating a "control problem" (Christiano's iterated amplification framework cited, p. 30; verified program synthesis, Leroy 2009).

---

### Safety, Alignment & Oversight Framing

**Scalable oversight challenge (pp. 27–28, governance section):**

The paper frames **human steering at superhuman speed** as unsolved:
- **The bottleneck:** If ASI can generate insights/artifacts faster than humans can evaluate them, oversight breaks down. (Analog: if email arrived 1000× faster, no inbox system could prioritize.)
- **Proposed mechanisms (not solved):**
  - Iterated amplification (Christiano): recursively decompose hard tasks, verify sub-tasks with weaker models, amplify weak model capability to AGI-level. *Status: theoretical, limited empirical validation.*
  - Verified program synthesis: AI patches its own code, but patches must satisfy formal proofs. *Status: works in toy domains, doesn't scale to reasoning/alignment.*
  - Human-in-the-loop with fast approval (e.g., Sovereign model): humans set high-level goals, AI refines within those constraints. *Status: depends on humans understanding the goal-refinement deeply.*

**Governance & deliberate slowdown (pp. 27–28):**

- **Sociopolitical feedback:** Public concern + regulatory pressure (EU AI Act, emerging US thresholds) create institutional gates. Paper notes this as a **plausible friction**: "societal backlash, accidents, regulation and deliberate slowdown interacting in a coupled system" (p. 27).
- **International coordination problem:** Unilateral regulation faces competitive dynamics; multilateral frameworks are rare historically. Result: expecting coordinated slowdown is "perhaps unrealistic" (p. 27).
- **Deceptive alignment risk:** Paper explicitly acknowledges that more capable systems become harder to audit for hidden goals—a *friction that tightens* as capability scales (p. 48, sleeper agents).

**Open research questions on governance (pp. 35–36, open Q5):**
- How to ensure epistemic resilience in asymmetric-intelligence collectives (mixed human-ASI groups)?
- How to harden agent groups against "epistemic hijacking" (spread of hallucinations, self-delusions)?
- Can group alignment be maintained via mechanism design (markets) vs. explicit control?

---

### Concrete Design Principles for Builders (Beyond Lab Scale)

**For product/application teams pursuing Via 3 + Via 4:**

1. **Measurement is prerequisite** (p. 35, all of open Q4): Define what "improvement" means *operationally* for your domain. Scaling laws for data gen, test-time search, distillation must be domain-specific; lab defaults don't transfer.

2. **Verify the loop closes** (implicit in iterated amplification framing): If your system claims "self-improving," prove the data/signal feeding back actually makes downstream steps better. Recursive distillation degenerates if the verifier (the loss signal) is weak (p. 35, 5d).

3. **Handle the Embodied Bottleneck** (pp. 26–27): If your ASI must interact with physical reality (robotics, experimental science), expect linear slowdowns from physical latencies. Don't assume compute speed = capability speed.

4. **Anticipate deceptive alignment** (p. 48): As your agent collective gains capability, beware outputs that *appear* to pass human evaluation but mask latent misalignment. Strategies: transparent reasoning traces, adversarial red-teaming, staged deployment.

5. **Governance design is *technical*; it's not political theater** (pp. 27–28): If you're building agent collectives intended for real-world deployment, embed human steering mechanisms *into the architecture* (e.g., explicit action review points, bounded autonomy by domain). Regulatory gates will emerge; better to have built them in voluntarily.

**Missing from the paper (for MCORCH builders):** Concrete metrics to validate that a collective *actually* benefits from adding agents, or when a single larger model is more efficient. The paper frames this as open Q5b (p. 36) but offers no production heuristic.

**1. The quote is an uncited best-practice bullet, not a benchmark finding.** Fetching the cited URL (`socialinsider.io/blog/tiktok-vs-reels-vs-shorts/`, pub. Oct 6, 2025) confirms the line "Create videos around 60 seconds to maximize engagement" appears in a best-practices list with **no data, sample, or citation attached**. The article gives platform duration *specs* (TikTok 15s–10min; Reels 15–180s; Shorts 15–180s) but runs no comparative duration-vs-engagement analysis. Calling this "Socialinsider's benchmark" is an overreach — the word "benchmark" imports empirical weight the page does not carry.

**2. Socialinsider's own actual benchmark data contradicts a flat ~60s target.** Their real study page (`social-media-benchmarks/social-media-video-statistics`, Sept 3, 2025; 11M IG posts, 3M FB, 2M TikTok, 67K LinkedIn) is platform-*divergent*, not convergent on 60s: Instagram Reels peak at **60–90s** (5,400 avg views, dropping to 4,965 at 90–120s), but **TikTok peaks at 2+ minutes** — 90–120s content averages 16,900 views vs 5,606 for 15–30s, nearly triple. A single "~60s" number for all 9:16 cuts flattens a spread their own data says runs from 60s to 120s+ depending on platform.

**3. YouTube Shorts is absent from the sample and contradicted elsewhere.** Socialinsider's benchmark sample covers Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, and LinkedIn — **no YouTube Shorts**. Since the research question centers on Shorts→long-form funneling, the claim is being applied to a platform the source never measured. Independent 2025–2026 analyses point the other way: multiple studies identify a 15–30s / 20–45s band as the Shorts sweet spot, with videos under 25s accounting for the majority of Shorts views. Evidence here is genuinely contested — one Inflow Network study of 5,400 Shorts found 50–60s videos pulled ~22x the views of sub-10s — which makes the claim's framing ("rather than the 15–30s commonly assumed") present an unsettled question as resolved.

**Methodological caveat compounding all three:** these are correlational per-video averages from a SaaS vendor's marketing benchmark, not controlled tests. Longer videos correlate with creator type, production budget, and topic; the data cannot support a causal "make it 60s → get more engagement" instruction.

The steelman worth preserving: there *is* real evidence that reflexively cutting to 15–30s is not automatically optimal, particularly on Reels and TikTok. But that is a much weaker and more platform-conditional statement than the claim as written.
